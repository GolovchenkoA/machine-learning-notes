Let’s quickly list some popular neural network architectures.

LeNet-5 is one of the earliest well-known neural network architectures. It was designed for document and handwriting recognition, especially handwritten digit recognition.

AlexNet is a convolutional neural network, or CNN, used for image recognition. It became influential after showing strong results on large-scale ImageNet classification.

ResNet is another popular CNN architecture. It introduced residual learning, which made it easier to train much deeper neural networks.

VGG is a class of CNN architectures known for using a simple and consistent design with small convolution filters across deeper networks.

LSTM (long short-term memory) is a recurrent neural network architecture used for sequence data. It is commonly applied to text, speech, time-series, and forecasting problems.

Transformers are the architecture behind many modern generative AI systems. They use attention mechanisms and remove the need for recurrence or convolutions in sequence processing.

Practical ways to reuse architectures
Depending on your use case, you can reuse architectures in different ways:

Use as-is when the pretrained model already solves your task
Fine-tune the model on your own dataset
Use for feature extraction and train another model on top
Modify the architecture for a related problem
Use prompting or RAG when working with foundation models
